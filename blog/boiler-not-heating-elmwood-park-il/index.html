Boiler Not Heating in Winter? What to Check Before You Call

Cold radiators on a January morning feel like an emergency. Some causes you can check safely in ten minutes, and some mean you should walk out and make a call.

By the Elmwood Park 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished October 29, 2026Updated October 29, 2026

The house is cold, the radiators are lukewarm at best, and the boiler in the basement is either silent or running without delivering heat. Before you pick up the phone, a short list of checks can tell you whether this is a quick fix or a job for a professional.

Many older homes use a boiler instead of a forced-air furnace, and the two main types behave very differently. This guide walks through safe checks for steam and hot-water systems, then draws a hard line around the things you should not touch. Our plumbers in Elmwood Park cover the water side of a boiler, and we say plainly where other trades take over.

Cold house, working boiler: what the symptoms usually mean

Start by describing what you actually see and hear. The pattern narrows the cause faster than any single test.

No heat and no sound

The boiler may have no power, be locked out, or be waiting on a thermostat that is not calling for heat.

Boiler runs but radiators stay cold

Air may be trapped, a circulator may have failed, or valves may be closed. Steam systems may also have a stuck vent.

Some radiators hot, others cold

Often trapped air in hot-water systems, or a blockage or closed valve to one zone.

Banging or knocking

Water pooled in a steam pipe or air in a hot-water loop. Not always dangerous, but it should be looked at.

Water on the floor near the boiler

A leak at a valve, joint or tank. Shut off any obvious supply you can reach and do not touch wet electrical gear.

Steam or hot water: know which system you have

Steam boiler

  • Heats water to make steam that rises through pipes to radiators.
  • Radiators have a small air vent on the side, and a sight glass on the boiler shows the water level.
  • Low water is a common cause of no heat or a shutdown.
  • Radiator valves should be fully open or fully closed, never partway.

Hot-water (hydronic) boiler

  • Heats water and sends it through pipes with a circulator pump.
  • Radiators have a small bleed valve at the top, and the boiler has a pressure gauge.
  • Trapped air and low pressure are common reasons for cold radiators.
  • Baseboard convectors and radiant loops often share the same boiler.

Not sure which you have? Look at the radiators. A large valve on one end and a small vent on the other suggests steam. A small bleed screw at the top suggests hot water. The nameplate on the boiler often says too.

Ten minutes of checks you can do safely

None of these involve opening the boiler, touching the burner or relighting anything. Stop at any point where something smells, looks scorched or feels beyond what you are comfortable doing.

Work down this list in order

  • 1Thermostat: Set it well above room temperature, check the mode says heat, and replace batteries if it has them.
  • 2Power: Look for the boiler switch, often a plain light-switch style box near the stairs or on the unit, and check the breaker.
  • 3Pressure gauge (hot-water systems): Compare the reading with the normal range printed on the manufacturer's label. A reading far below it can stop heating.
  • 4Water level (steam systems): Check the sight glass against the marking on the boiler. If it looks empty, do not add water unless you know the procedure.
  • 5Radiator valves: Make sure each valve is fully open. Steam valves left partway can hiss, spit or stay cold.
  • 6Leaks: Look at the floor and at joints, valves and the relief valve. Dampness or rust stains deserve a call.

Bleeding air from hot-water radiators

If the top of a radiator stays cold while the bottom is warm, air is probably trapped inside. Turn the heating off for a bit so the pump is not pushing water around, place a rag and a small container under the bleed valve, and open it a quarter turn with a radiator key or a small flat screwdriver.

You will hear a hiss. Close the valve the moment a steady stream of water appears. Then check the pressure gauge again, because bleeding air lets some water out and the system may need topping up. Adding water is a step many homeowners prefer to hand to a plumber.

Do not bleed steam radiators the same way. Their vents work differently, and opening one can release hot steam. If a steam radiator is cold, check the vent and the valve, and call for help if neither explains it.

Stop and leave: gas smell, CO alarm, fumes

If you smell gas, leave the building immediately. Do not flip switches or use a phone inside. From outside, call the gas utility and 911. Nicor Gas serves Elmwood Park, though some addresses may use a different utility, so check your bill.

A carbon monoxide alarm that sounds means the same thing: get everyone outside, then call 911. Never relight a pilot you do not understand, never reset a boiler repeatedly after it locks out, and never ignore soot, scorching or a flue pipe that has come loose.

When frozen pipes make a cold house colder

In severe cold, the heating loop is not the only thing at risk. Pipes in an unheated basement, crawl space or outside wall can freeze, and a frozen pipe can split. If your boiler is down during a hard freeze, keep the house as warm as you can and open cabinet doors on exterior walls.

If a pipe has burst, shut off the main water valve and call us right away. Our burst pipe repair team deals with winter breaks regularly. For a boiler that has stopped but is not leaking, our boiler repair page explains what we handle on the water side.
