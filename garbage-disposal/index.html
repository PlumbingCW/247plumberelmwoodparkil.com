Short answer: do not put your hand in it. If the disposal hums and will not spin, or the sink is draining slowly and the cabinet is wet, turn the switch off, and unplug the unit or switch off its breaker before anything else. Our 24/7 line is open for disposal problems in Elmwood Park.

A garbage disposal is a small grinder bolted under the sink drain, with a motor below and a drain port that carries the waste into the trap. Most failures are a jam, a leak at a seal or flange, or a unit that has simply worn out.

Emergency triage for a jammed or leaking disposal

1

Is the switch off and the power cut?

Wall switch off first. If you can see an outlet or plug under the sink, unplug it. A hardwired unit needs its breaker turned off.

2

Does it hum, click or stay silent?

Humming means the motor has power but the blades are jammed. Silent usually means a tripped reset or a dead circuit.

3

Where does the water appear?

A drip from the top is the flange. A drip from the side is the dishwasher hose or a drain connection. A drip from the bottom usually means the housing has failed.

4

Does the sink drain on its own?

If water sits in the basin, the problem may be the trap or drain line beyond the disposal, not the unit.

What each sound or symptom tells us

Disposals do not fail at random. Each symptom points at a specific part.

Loud hum, no spin

A hard object or stuck blade. Many models have a hex socket on the bottom for turning the shaft by hand, once the power is off.

Dead silence

A tripped overload reset, a tripped breaker, or a failed motor. The red reset button on the bottom is the first stop.

Grinding with metal rattle

A spoon, bottle cap or fork has fallen into the grind chamber. Fish it out with tongs, never fingers.

Water pooling at the bottom

Seals between the upper and lower housing wear out. That kind of leak means the unit is finished.

Foul smell that returns

Food residue builds up on the splash guard and baffle. A cleaning may help, but a persistent smell can mean a partly blocked drain line.

Repair it or replace the disposal

Repairing fits when

  • A jam can be freed without damage to the motor
  • The leak comes from a loose drain connection or a hose
  • The reset button restores power and it runs normally
  • The unit is fairly new and the housing is dry

Replacing fits when

  • The housing leaks from the body
  • The motor hums, resets and trips again
  • Rust shows near the bottom plate or the seams
  • The disposal is old and repairs would cost more than a new one

The dishwasher connection most people forget

Many dishwashers drain through the disposal. A small knockout plug inside the disposal's side port must be removed when a dishwasher is connected, and if it is left in place the dishwasher stops draining. That is a common reason a new install backs up into the dishwasher.

When a disposal is replaced, the dishwasher hose is reconnected with a clamp, and the line should rise in a high loop under the counter so dirty water cannot flow back. We check that loop on every job.

A disposal handles soft food scraps with plenty of cold water. These items cause most of the service calls we describe above.

The electrical part is not a plumber's job

Connecting a disposal to a plug or hardwired circuit, adding an outlet under the sink or a new wall switch is electrical work. We handle the plumbing side. For any new circuit, outlet or switch, hire a licensed electrician.

Is it the disposal, or the drain beyond it?

Water that backs up into the sink even with the unit off usually means a blocked trap or branch drain. Replacing the disposal will not change that. We check the trap, the line to the wall and the vent stack before assuming the unit is guilty.

In older multi-unit buildings, a kitchen branch can be shared with units above or below, so a blockage can show up in a neighbor's sink first. Our drain cleaning team clears the line with a cable machine, and a camera finds the cause when the problem keeps coming back.
