Tankless Water Heater in Elmwood Park, IL

A tankless water heater heats water only when a tap opens, using a burner or electric elements inside a compact box. That is why tankless water heater service in Elmwood Park is mostly about what the unit needs from the house: gas volume, venting, or a strong electrical service.

We install tankless units, repair them, and flush the scale that builds up inside the heat exchanger. If your unit is flashing a code or the water goes cold midway through a shower, this page tells you what is likely happening. A failed old tank is covered under water heater repair.

Does a tankless unit suit your house?

The unit is only half the equation. Gas models need a large enough gas line and meter, and the vent must carry exhaust outside. In older homes the existing gas pipe may be sized for a small tank, not a unit that fires hard for a few minutes.

Electric models draw a lot of power, and a licensed electrician must assess the panel and wiring. We do not give electrical specifications, because the right answer depends on your service capacity and the unit chosen.

How a descaling flush works

1

Isolate the unit

We shut off the gas or power, close the supply valves and open the service ports.

2

Circulate solution

A small pump moves a descaling solution through the heat exchanger for a set time.

3

Rinse and clean

We rinse the unit, then clean the inlet filter.

4

Test and record

We fire the unit, check the temperature and flow, and clear any scale-related faults.

How often to flush

A yearly flush is the common rule of thumb, and it matters more where the water leaves mineral deposits. Hardness is not stated in the Village water report, so if you are unsure, check the report or test your tap. A flush costs far less than a failed heat exchanger.

When tankless is not worth it

A good fit

  • A large household with long, heavy hot water use
  • A home with a gas line and vent that can be upgraded
  • Someone who wants to gain floor space
  • A plan to flush the unit yearly

A weak fit

  • A small household with short, scattered use
  • A line too small and costly to enlarge
  • No easy way to vent a high-output burner
  • A panel that cannot carry an electric model

A standard tank is still the simpler path in many homes. Read about planned water heater installation to compare.

Repairs we handle on tankless units

Some jobs belong to the manufacturer, and some do not. These are the ones a plumber can usually finish on site.

Ignition and flame faults

Igniter, flame rod and sensors that fail or get dirty.

Venting problems

Blocked, cracked or poorly pitched vent pipes.

Flow and temperature issues

Inlet screens, flow sensors and mixing issues.

Leaks

Fittings, isolation valves and condensate lines. A leaking heat exchanger usually means replacement.

Isolation valves

If your unit was installed without service valves, we can add them so a flush is possible.

Cold weather and older basements

A winter low of minus 23 F at O'Hare in January 2019 showed how hard Chicago-area cold can hit. Condensate lines and exterior vent terminations are the parts of a tankless unit most exposed to that kind of freeze.

With a median home built around 1955, many Elmwood Park basements carry older gas piping and tight chimneys. A gas odor anywhere in the house means getting outside and phoning the utility or 911 before anything else.

Pricing without surprises

We do not name a price for a tankless job before a visit. For installs, gas line size, vent length, the unit's capacity and whether electrical work is needed drive the cost. For repairs, the failed part and access decide it.

You get the price before work starts. If a tank swap is more sensible, we say so, and you can read more at water heater replacement.
