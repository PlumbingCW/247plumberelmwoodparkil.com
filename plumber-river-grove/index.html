River first

What the Des Plaines River has done to River Grove

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan lists flood events for River Grove in 1986, 1987, 1995, 1997, 2008, 2010, 2011 and 2013. It says flooding at Grand Avenue and River Road, and at Fullerton Avenue and River Road, has made those areas impassable. The same plan lists 25 repetitive loss properties in the village.

A flooded street can slow any truck, so know your shutoffs before water arrives. And when the river is high, groundwater pressure rises under basement floors, which is when sump pumps and floor drains get tested.

The plan also lists planned actions along River Road, including property acquisition and a permanent street gate closure. Those are plans, not finished work, so your own sump, check valves and drain lines still do the protecting.

Corridors and neighborhoods we know in River Grove

These are places named in the county plan's history of the village. Each one comes with a plumbing reason to care.

Grand Avenue and River Road

The Grand Avenue bridge carries traffic over the Des Plaines River, and the intersections near it are the ones the county plan names for flooding.

Chicago Home Gardens

This subdivision dates to 1920 and lies west of Elmwood Park. Older lines here are often cast iron or clay tile. A sewer camera shows what is down there.

Rhodes Subdivision

Platted in 1872 on the south side of Grand Avenue, it is among the oldest parts of the village. Low pressure or a wet yard here is worth a proper line check.

Turner Park and the old Cazenovia crossing

Turner Park is the area east of the river, and Cazenovia was the original name of the settlement at the crossing. Low ground near water is where a failed sump shows up first.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

Four steps limit damage in the first few minutes. None need tools.

1

Shut the main valve

Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops. It is usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.

2

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, do not step in it. Switch off a breaker only if you can reach it dry.

3

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the water's path.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Tell us where the water is. Our plumbers are dispatched around the clock, and we will give you a clear price before work starts. If you smell gas, leave first.

Sewer backups and floor drains in a river village

When a floor drain burps or a toilet gurgles, the first question is whether the blockage is in your own line or somewhere beyond it. Our plumbers start with a drain machine and cable, and a sewer camera when it keeps returning. See our drain cleaning and sewer line cleaning pages.

River Grove's stormwater runs on a separate system, but we do not assume anything about the sanitary pipe under your house. The camera shows what is really there. A check valve or backwater valve is often the fix when backups repeat.

Permits go through River Grove, not Elmwood Park

River Grove has its own village hall on Thatcher Avenue and its own building department. Its page says remodeling over a small dollar amount needs a building permit, and that plumbing permits require a Letter of Intent from the contractor. That is separate from Elmwood Park's process.

Which jobs need a permit varies, so ask the River Grove building department before a water heater swap, a sewer repair or a sump install. Always confirm a plumber is licensed through the Illinois Department of Public Health lookup, which lists plumbing licenses.

Chicago water from the Village reservoir

River Grove buys Lake Michigan water from the City of Chicago, stores it in an underground reservoir and pumps it out to homes. The 2024 water quality report publishes no hardness figure, so test your tap if scale bothers you.

Slow flow and tired tanks are common complaints. See water heater repair if your tank leaks or runs cold.
