A business emergency is not a good moment for a lecture. We ask what is flooding, whether there is sewage, whether electrical equipment is wet, and whether the building can stay open. The answers decide the first step: isolate, protect, then repair.

After-hours work means a plumber with a drain machine, a camera and the tools to cut and replace pipe. We explain the plan and the price before work starts, then fix the problem or stabilize it so the doors can open on time.

Floor drains and restroom fixtures in daily use

Floor drains collect water but they also collect debris. A drain that has not been flushed in months may have a dry trap that lets sewer gas into the room. Pouring water into a seldom-used drain restores the seal.

Commercial toilets and urinals use flush valves, not tank flappers. They need clean water, steady pressure and periodic service. A fixture that runs constantly wastes water, and a toilet that clogs repeatedly points to a main or branch line problem. See drain cleaning and sewer line cleaning for the methods we use.

Grease traps and interceptors: what we handle and what we do not

A grease trap is a tank that catches fat before it enters the sewer. Pumping a full trap is usually a hauling job done with a vacuum truck. Our plumbers handle the plumbing around it: clearing lines that feed it, finding why it backs up, and repairing or replacing fittings and baffles.

If you need the tank pumped, ask us on the phone whether we do it for your size of trap. We will tell you plainly, and if it is outside our work, we will say so rather than leave you waiting.

Backflow prevention: testing and repair, hedged honestly

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that keeps dirty water from flowing back into the clean supply. Required testing, and who can do it, is set by the local water authority. Ask us on the phone whether we can test or service your particular assembly.

Repair or replacement of a failed assembly is plumbing work, and we can talk through it. If your utility sent a notice, read it, and tell us what it says so we can answer correctly.

Price depends on access, hours, pipe size and material, the type of equipment needed, and whether permits or inspections are part of the work. We give a clear price before work starts, and we separate emergency stabilization from larger follow-up repairs.
