Why older Elmwood Park homes call for a careful plumber

Elmwood Park grew fast after the Second World War, and the median home in the village was built in 1955. Roughly three out of four homes here predate 1970, and about 29 percent were built before 1940. That mix means original cast iron drains, clay sewer laterals and old galvanized supply lines are still in service in many houses.

Old pipe fails in predictable ways. Galvanized steel narrows from the inside until the shower is a trickle. Cast iron scales and catches grease. Clay tile joints let tree roots in. A plumber who knows what a 70 year old house usually hides can diagnose faster and repair the right section instead of guessing.

Half of the housing is single-family detached, and the rest is split among two-flats, small walk-up buildings and larger apartment buildings. If you manage a multi-unit building, our commercial plumbing page explains how we handle after-hours calls.

Heavy rain, combined sewers and basement backups

Elmwood Park is served by combined sewers, which means storm water and household waste share the same pipe under the street. When a storm overloads that pipe, water can push back up through basement drains. In July 2011, rain of two to four inches an hour left several feet of water on Oak Leaf Avenue, and the county later recorded a flood mitigation project completed in 2015 to reduce sewer backups.

If water is coming up a floor drain, stop using water in the house and call us. We can tell you whether your own line is blocked or the street sewer is overloaded, and we can talk through long-term protection such as an overhead sewer, a backwater valve or a sump pump with battery backup.

Permits, licences and what to ask any plumber

Illinois requires plumbers to be licensed through the Department of Public Health, and the licence number must appear in plumbing advertising. The state publishes an online lookup, so you can check a name and number in a minute before anyone starts work.

The Village of Elmwood Park building department handles plumbing permits and inspections. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the building department what your project needs. Permits are paid before work starts, and inspections need two working days of notice, so planned work such as a water heater installation or a repipe goes more smoothly when scheduled ahead.

Winter, frozen pipes and what the cold does

Chicago-area cold is hard on plumbing. O'Hare dropped to minus 23 degrees Fahrenheit on January 30, 2019, and minus 8 on December 23, 2022. Exterior wall pipes, garage supply lines and unheated crawl spaces freeze first, and a frozen pipe that splits will not show itself until it thaws.

If you suspect a freeze, open the nearest faucet slightly, keep the heat on and call early. A thawed pipe that is already split is a burst pipe repair, and finding the main shutoff before that happens saves a lot of damage.
