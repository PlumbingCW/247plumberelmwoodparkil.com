A boiler heats water, or makes steam, and sends it through pipes to radiators or baseboard. We repair the plumbing side of that system: leaks, pressure, pumps, valves, air and expansion. Problems with the burner, flue or gas valve are different, and we are direct about where our work stops.

Safety before repairs

Carbon monoxide comes from combustion. Keep working CO alarms near sleeping areas and outside the boiler room. If an alarm sounds, leave and call 911. A gas smell means everyone leaves, and the utility or 911 is the first call. Never relight a boiler that smells of gas or smoke.

Steam or hot water: the first thing to know about your system

Steam boilers

  • Water boils into steam, which rises to radiators
  • Pipes pitch back toward the boiler so condensate returns
  • A sight glass shows the water level
  • Banging pipes often mean trapped water

Hot water (hydronic) boilers

  • Heated water is pushed through pipes by a circulator pump
  • A pressure gauge and expansion tank manage the water
  • Radiators or baseboard may need bleeding of air
  • Cold spots often mean air or a stuck valve

Check the label or ask us. The repair path is different for each type, and it shapes the questions we ask on the phone. Our service area covers Elmwood Park and nearby towns.

What we fix

No heat from the radiators: the water-side causes we can solve

Several common problems sit squarely in the plumbing and do not involve the burner.

Low or swinging pressure

A leaking relief valve, failed fill valve or waterlogged expansion tank often shows on the gauge.

Cold radiators

Air in the lines or a stuck zone valve keeps hot water from reaching them.

Circulator pump noise or stalling

A worn pump or seized motor stops water from moving even when the boiler is hot.

Puddle at the boiler

A weeping fitting, valve packing or pipe joint. We trace the source before repairs.

Low-water cutoff trips

A fouled or failed cutoff shuts the boiler down. We test and replace it where it sits in the water-side controls.

Five checks to make before the plumber arrives

These are safe for most homes and give us a better starting point.

Quick homeowner checks

  • 1Thermostat. Raise it a few degrees and confirm it is set to heat with fresh batteries if it needs them.
  • 2Power switch. Many boilers have a switch near the unit or on the stairs. Make sure nobody turned it off.
  • 3Pressure gauge. Note the number. Do not open valves to change it.
  • 4Water on the floor. Look for drips and tell us where you see them.
  • 5Error light or code. Write down what the display shows.

Older Elmwood Park homes and the boilers they were built around

With many homes built before 1970, and about 29 percent before 1940, steam and hot water boilers still serve some Elmwood Park houses and apartment buildings. Original iron and galvanized piping tend to carry sediment that clogs valves and wears pumps.

Two-flats and larger buildings are common here, and a shared boiler can mean several households are cold at once. Tell us the building layout when you call, and mention any water that has spread, since a leak detection visit can trace a hidden source. When the cold is the emergency

Chicago saw minus 23 F at O'Hare on January 30, 2019, and the county plan notes frozen pipes in extreme cold. A boiler that stops in that weather puts every pipe in the house at risk, so call early and keep taps open slightly on exterior walls.
