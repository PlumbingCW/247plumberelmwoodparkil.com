Sewage in the tub, toilets that gurgle, water creeping out along basement baseboards: the main line is not carrying waste out of the house. Our 24/7 line takes these calls at any hour. Stop all water use, do not flush, and keep everyone out of the wet area, especially near outlets and appliances.

Sewer line cleaning is the clearing of the main drain that carries every fixture's waste from your house to the street sewer, usually through a cleanout with a heavy cable and a camera. In Elmwood Park the line is often older clay or cast iron, which makes roots and buildup common causes.

Sewage backing up right now: the first moves

Most of the damage in a backup comes from more water being added. These steps limit it while you wait for us.

Do this, in this order

  • 1Stop every drop of water. No flushing, laundry, dishwasher or showers. A full main line has nowhere to send it.
  • 2Stay out of the standing water. Sewage carries bacteria. If water is near outlets, a panel or plugged-in appliances, stay back and tell us on the phone.
  • 3Leave the cleanout cap alone. If the line is full, opening it can release a rush of backed-up sewage onto the floor.
  • 4Move belongings up. Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off a basement floor if you can do it safely.
  • 5Take photos, then call your insurer. Ask whether your policy covers sewer backup, because many do not unless it was added.

Main line or branch line: reading the pattern

Branch line clog

  • One fixture or one bathroom is affected
  • Other drains in the house run normally
  • A cable at that fixture usually settles it

Main line clog

  • Several fixtures slow or back up together
  • The lowest drain in the house gurgles or rises first
  • Flushing or running the washer makes another drain bubble

A good rule: the lowest opening in the house tells the story. A basement floor drain or a ground-floor tub that fills when an upstairs fixture runs is a strong sign that the line below has closed. For a single stubborn fixture, see drain cleaning.

Where the cleanout is, and why it matters

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives a plumber a straight way into the main line without pulling a toilet. Locating it saves time on a night call, so it is worth finding before you need it.

Common places to look:

  • In the basement floor or low on a wall, near where the main leaves the house
  • In a crawlspace or utility room close to the foundation
  • Outside, in a front or side yard, in a small capped pipe at ground level

If you cannot find one, say so when you call. We can often work through a toilet or a floor drain, though a house with no cleanout may benefit from having one added.

Combined sewers in Elmwood Park: whose pipe is it?

The Village is served by combined sewers, which means rain and household waste share one pipe, owned and maintained by the Village. That shapes how a backup should be read.

Your side of the line

A clog between your house and the point where your line meets the street sewer is generally the owner's pipe, though you should check with the Village. This is what we clear.

The street sewer

The large pipe under the street belongs to the Village. If our camera shows your line is clear and the water is still coming up, we say so, and you can report it.

Heavy rain surcharge

In a downpour, the combined sewer can fill and push water back into basements. That can happen with a perfectly clear home line, and a cable will not stop it.

The long-term fix

A backwater valve or an overhead sewer is how a house protects itself from rain surcharge. The Village has offered a flood-proofing assistance program, so ask the building department if it is open. For related pump work, see sump pump service.

Why main lines clog again: roots, grease and the pipe itself

A main line that opens and then closes again has a reason. Roots enter old clay and iron joints, grease builds where lines run cool, and flushed wipes snag on anything rough. A line that sags will hold paper and catch it again.

A camera pass tells us which one we are dealing with. Where buildup is the cause, hydro jetting cleans the wall more fully than a cable. Where the pipe is breaking, cleaning only buys time, and sewer line repair is the real answer.
