One buried pipe carries every gallon your house uses, and nobody ever sees it. When that service line fails in Elmwood Park, the 24/7 line is open. If water is surfacing in the yard or the whole house has lost pressure, close the house main valve, stay off any soft ground near the leak, and note whether the meter is still turning.

Water line repair means fixing or replacing the service line, the pipe that runs from the water main in the street, past the meter and into the house. The Village buys Lake Michigan water from Chicago, meters it and pumps it to homes, so this line is where public water becomes yours.

Signs the problem is the line under the yard, not the plumbing inside

Pressure falls everywhere at once

Every tap, the shower and the washer all weaken together. A single weak fixture usually points to that fixture instead.

A soggy or sunken spot

A wet patch, a dip in the lawn or a strip of unusually green grass can mark the path of the pipe.

The meter runs with everything off

Movement while the house is quiet points to a leak somewhere past the meter. See leak detection for how to check.

No water at all

If neighbors still have water, the line to your house may have broken or the main valve closed. If neighbors are dry too, that is a Village matter.

Rushing sound near the entry wall

A hiss or rush where the pipe comes through the foundation suggests a leak or a failing joint at that spot.

How a service line job unfolds, from the 811 call to the flush

1

Confirm the line is the culprit

We close valves, read the meter and listen along the pipe, so we are not digging up a yard for a fixture problem.

2

Call 811 before any digging

Illinois law requires a JULIE locate at least 3 days and no more than 10 before digging, at any depth, at no charge. Marks last 25 days. Utilities do not mark private lines, so we locate those ourselves.

3

Open the ground carefully

We excavate to the pipe, by hand near other utilities, and keep a path for water to drain from the trench.

4

Join, test and flush

New pipe goes in with proper fittings, the line is pressure-tested, and the system is flushed before water returns to your taps.

5

Backfill and restore

We return the soil and compact it. Lawn, concrete and driveway finishing may be separate trades.

Repair a section or replace the whole line

A spot repair makes sense when

  • One break sits in an otherwise sound, modern pipe.
  • The pipe is accessible and the surrounding line is in good shape.
  • No lead is involved.
  • The line has not failed before.

Full replacement makes sense when

  • The pipe is galvanized steel, corroded or has failed more than once.
  • The line is lead, or its material is unknown and old.
  • Multiple leaks or weak spots are found while digging.
  • Pressure has declined for years and the pipe interior is rough.

Lead changes the picture. Illinois law bars partial lead service line replacement in general, so a repair that touches a lead line may turn into a full replacement.

Lead service lines: the Village inventory and State law

Many older lines across the country were made of lead, and the State now requires communities to track them.

The Village survey

The Village is surveying water service materials under the Illinois Lead Service Line Replacement and Notification Act, and asks owners and tenants to answer its Water Service Material Inventory.

The inventory

Ask the Village for a copy. It also says it has started planning for lead service removal over the next few years.

State law on partial replacement

Illinois generally bars replacing only part of a lead line, so a repair is not always the shortcut it seems.

What we do not do

We do not guess your pipe material from the street. The material is confirmed by looking at the line itself, or from Village records.

How deep a line has to be, and why shallow ones fail in winter

The Illinois plumbing code sets a minimum burial depth for a water service of 36 inches or the local frost depth, whichever is greater. A line laid shallower, or one that settled with age, can freeze in a hard cold snap.

O'Hare reached minus 23 degrees on January 30, 2019. A line near the surface, or one passing under a driveway apron, is where trouble starts. When we replace a line, we bury it to the required depth.

Replacing a line without trenching the whole yard

Trenchless methods pull or push a new pipe through the path of the old one, working from a small pit at each end. That can spare a lawn, a walk or a driveway, but it needs a suitable path, and not every line qualifies. We look at what is under your yard before suggesting it.

Who owns the pipe, and what the Village may require

Ownership of the service line usually divides somewhere along its length, but the exact point depends on the Village, so ask Village public works before assuming. Do not dig near the street or the curb stop on your own.

Plumbers working in the Village file a State licence, proof of liability insurance and a bond, and a permit is paid before the job starts. Whether yours needs one depends on the work, so ask the building department. Inspections need two working days notice.

What drives the price of a service line job

Length, depth, pipe material, what sits above the line (lawn, concrete, a tree), whether it is a repair or full replacement, and permit and inspection steps all shape the total. You get a clear price before work starts.
