Emergency Sewer Line Repair in Elmwood Park, IL

Cleaning clears a pipe, and repair mends one that is broken. Our 24/7 line is open when a cracked or collapsed sewer pipe is sending sewage back into the house.

If sewage keeps coming up and a cable only buys a few days, the pipe itself may be broken. Our 24/7 line takes these calls at night and on weekends. Until we are there, stop using water, do not flush, and keep people away from wet floors, outlets and appliances.

Sewer line repair means fixing the pipe that carries waste from your house to the street sewer, whether by replacing a short section, lining the pipe from the inside, or replacing the run. In Elmwood Park, where most homes are 50 to 90 years old, the pipe is often clay tile or cast iron that has aged.

When sewage keeps returning, the pipe may be broken

A line that clogs once is a cleaning job. A line that clogs in the same place again and again, or one that backs up soon after being cleared, is a candidate for repair. The cable is clearing the symptom while the pipe keeps catching waste.

Signs that point toward a pipe problem:

  • The same clog returns in weeks, in the same fixtures
  • The camera or cable stops at a sag, an offset joint or a break
  • A sunken strip or soggy patch in the yard along the line
  • A sewer smell in the basement or yard that keeps coming back
  • Roots in the line every time it is cleaned

We start with a camera inspection so the repair matches what is actually down there. A line that only needs clearing may be a hydro jetting job instead.

Spot repair, pipe lining or full replacement

The right repair depends on how much of the pipe is bad, how deep it lies and what is on top of it.

Repair methods and their trade-offs
MethodFits whenTrade-offs
Spot repairOne short bad section, such as a broken joint or crushed pieceNeeds a dig at that point, and the rest of the old pipe stays
Pipe lining (trenchless)Cracks or root entry in a pipe that is still round and in placeCannot fix a collapsed or badly offset pipe, and reduces the bore slightly

Cast iron and clay tile: how old sewer pipes fail

Cast iron

  • Rusts and scales from the inside, narrowing the bore
  • Can flake into layers and crack at joints
  • Heavy and often found under slabs and in basements

Clay tile

  • Made in short lengths, so there are many joints for roots to enter
  • Can crack or shift with soil movement
  • Common in older underground runs to the street

Nearly 3 in 10 homes in the village were built before 1940, and about 77 percent before 1970, so these materials are often the ones we meet. Roots are the usual culprit in clay, and lining or replacement is how that gets fixed for good.

Digging means calling 811 first

Illinois law requires calling JULIE at 811 at least 3 days, and no more than 10, before you dig. The call costs nothing, and the locates last 25 days. Utilities do not mark a private sewer lateral, so our plumbers locate that line themselves.

Who owns the pipe, and who signs off the work

The owner is usually responsible for the sewer lateral on their own property, though you should check with the Village for how that applies to your address. The street sewer is a Village matter.

What a given repair needs from the Village depends on the work, so the building department is the place to ask. It requires plumbers to hold a State licence, insurance and a bond, and inspections need two working days notice. Any plumber's licence can be checked on the Illinois Department of Public Health lookup.

What moves the price of a sewer repair

We give a clear price before work starts. The big drivers are how much pipe is bad, how deep it is, what sits on top (lawn, driveway, slab or garage), the pipe material and size, and whether the Village needs a permit or an inspection. Lining and digging cost differently, and neither is automatically the cheaper path.
