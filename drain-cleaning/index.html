Emergency Drain Cleaning in Elmwood Park, IL

Standing water in a sink, tub or floor drain is not something to live with overnight. Our licensed plumbers clear it, on the 24/7 line, with the right machine for the pipe.

A sink that will not drain, a tub holding gray water, a washer that quit mid-cycle with a puddle on the floor: this is what we clear every day, and the 24/7 line is answered at night and on weekends. While you wait, stop running water into the blocked fixture, switch the washer off, and lift anything stored near a basement drain.

Drain cleaning is the removal of a blockage from a household waste pipe, usually with a drain machine, which is a motor-driven cable fitted with cutting or retrieval heads. Many Elmwood Park homes are decades old, so we see everything from a hair clog at a pop-up stopper to scale and roots deep in a branch line.

Which drain is clogged tells you where the clog sits

Water only lingers where something is holding it back. Notice which fixtures are affected and which are fine, because that pattern points at the clog before any tool comes out of the truck.

Kitchen sink

Grease, coffee grounds and food scraps form a paste on the pipe wall below the basket. The sink drains in slow gulps, then stops. A disposal that hums but leaves water standing points to the same buildup.

Tub or shower

Hair wraps around the stopper or strainer, then binds with soap film farther down. A slow tub next to a healthy sink tells us the clog is on that one branch.

Washing machine standpipe

The pump moves water out fast, so a partly blocked line overflows the standpipe near the end of the cycle. Lint and detergent residue are the usual cause.

Basement floor drain

A floor drain that fills when the washer runs, or after a hard rain, is telling you the line it joins is full. Gray water suggests a clog. A sewage smell suggests something deeper.

Bathroom sink

Hair and toothpaste residue collect at the stopper and the trap. Often this needs a trap cleaning, not a full cable run, and it is worth saying so on the phone.

When a slow drain turns into a backup in the house

One slow fixture is a branch problem. Two or three at once is different. If a toilet gurgles when the washer drains, the shower fills when you flush, or a basement drain bubbles while someone upstairs runs a tap, the shared line below them is restricted, and that is main line territory.

Hold off on laundry, dishwasher cycles and showers while you wait. Every gallon sent down a blocked line has to surface somewhere, and the lowest opening in the house is usually a basement drain or a tub. We offer same-day appointments when available, and for the main line itself see sewer line cleaning.

What a drain machine reaches that a store snake does not

Hardware-store hand snake

  • A thin cable, often 15 to 25 feet, turned by hand
  • Tends to poke a hole through a clog and leave the rest on the pipe wall
  • Can kink or snap, leaving its tip in the line
  • Fine for hair in a bathroom sink

Our drain machine

  • Motor-driven cable in several thicknesses, fed through a cleanout when there is one
  • Cutting heads that shred grease and roots, retrieval heads that pull out a rag or a toy
  • Reaches past the trap and the first bend into the branch or main line
  • Paired with a drain camera when the cause is unclear

Many jobs we take over began with a store snake that stopped at a bend or a damaged section. Tell us where it stopped, because that point is useful information.

Chemical drain cleaners: why we do not begin there

Liquid cleaners work through heat and caustic chemistry. On a standing-water clog they sit in the pipe, soften what they touch, and leave a hazardous pool for the next person to work in. If you have already poured some, wear gloves and eye protection near the drain and tell us the product name when you call.

They also do little against hair, roots or a wad of cloth. A cable with the right head removes the clog rather than dissolving a corner of it.

If it clogs again, put a camera on it before the next cable

A drain that clears and then slows within weeks has a cause the cable never touched. A drain camera, a small lens on a flexible rod, shows what a cable cannot: a sagging stretch holding water, a cracked joint, roots entering at a seam, scale or grease coating the wall.

Age makes this a fair first guess. The median home in the village dates to 1955, so original cast iron, clay and galvanized drains are often still in service, and pipe that old scales on the inside and shifts at its joints. When the camera becomes the main job, see sewer inspection.

Storm drains, briefly

A storm drain is the inlet that carries rainwater away, in the street or in a yard. Street inlets connect to the Village-owned sewer, so a blocked one is reported to the Village. Yard inlets and downspout drains on your lot fall under the yard drain line in the table above.

Here, rain and household waste share combined sewers. An inlet that will not empty in a downpour can therefore be a capacity problem and not a clog, which a cable would not change.

What moves the price of a drain job

You get a clear price before work starts, and it turns on things you can describe over the phone. These include which fixture, how far from the opening the clog sits, whether a cleanout allows access or the fixture must come off, the pipe size, and whether a camera is needed. A trap cleaning is a smaller job than a long cable run into a branch line.

When scale or grease keeps winning, hydro jetting may fit better than a cable, and we will say so before you pay for the wrong tool.
