A backup that returns after a snake has been through is the usual reason people search for hydro jetting. Hydro jetting, also spelled hydrojetting and sometimes called water jetting, sends water at high pressure through a hose and a nozzle that scours the inside of a drain or sewer pipe. In Elmwood Park it is a planned fix for lines that clog again and again, and a poor choice for pipe that is already damaged.

Snaked it and it clogged again: what the repeat is telling you

A clog that keeps coming back is seldom bad luck. How soon it returns usually points at the cause.

Back within days or weeks

A cable opened a narrow path through a thick coat of grease. The path closes as warm water cools and the coating builds again. Jetting removes the coat itself.

Back every year or two

Roots are a strong suspect. Fine root hairs find a joint, feed on the water, and thicken until the pipe barely passes flow.

Slow but never fully stopped

Scale. Mineral and rust deposits narrow the bore from the walls inward, especially in older iron, so water moves like it is going through a smaller pipe.

The snake stopped at one spot

That may be a bend, a broken section or a sagging stretch and not a clog. A camera comes before any jet in this case.

Only the kitchen line

Cooking fat is the usual culprit. A jet can clean a long kitchen branch more fully than a cable that tunnels through the middle.

Roots, grease and scale are three different jobs

The nozzle matters as much as the pressure. A forward nozzle breaks a hard blockage so a rear-facing one can follow and clean the walls. A spinning root head cuts roots back to the pipe wall. Scale and grease respond to a fan or rotating pattern that scrubs along the length.

Each cause leaves a different mark on the camera, and that is why the visual check matters:

  • Grease shows as a pale, thick lining that narrows the bore evenly.
  • Roots show as fine threads or a mat, usually entering at a joint.
  • Scale shows as rough, crusted rings, often brown or orange in iron.

Roots that are cut but not stopped will grow back, so a root line is an upkeep schedule and not a one-time fix. For stubborn root entry at a joint, sewer line repair may be the lasting answer.

How a jetting visit runs

1

Camera look first

A sewer camera goes in to see the pipe, the buildup and any damage. If the pipe looks wrong for pressure, we stop and discuss it before any water goes in.

2

Set up at the cleanout

We feed the jetting hose through a cleanout or an opened access point and protect the floor and fixtures nearby.

3

Work the line in passes

The nozzle goes in short of the blockage, breaks through, then is drawn back to scrub the wall on the return pass.

4

Look again and report

A second camera pass shows how the pipe looks. We tell you what the screen showed and whether another visit is likely.

A backup tonight: why the cable may come first

Hydro jetting is usually planned work. When sewage or gray water is coming into the house at night, the priority is getting flow back, and a drain machine can often do that faster than setting up a jetter. We will tell you plainly which tool fits the emergency and which fits the repeat problem.

Once the house is usable again, a jetting visit can be scheduled and the camera review done calmly. We offer same-day appointments when available, including after hours. For a general stoppage, drain cleaning covers the cable side.

Camera first: cracked clay and bellied pipe

Jetting is the wrong tool where the pipe itself is failing. Water at pressure can wash soil out through a crack in clay tile, enlarging a hole that was small. A bellied section, a low sag that holds water, will not be cured by cleaning because the pipe is shaped to hold water.

In both cases the camera finds the problem, and the fix is a repair, not a jet.

Safety steps around a high-pressure jet

The pressure that cleans a pipe can hurt a person. These are the ground rules for the day of the visit.

Please keep these in mind

  • 1Stay clear of the hose. A loose or running hose can whip. Leave it to the plumber.
  • 2Keep hands and feet away from the nozzle. Water at this pressure can cut skin even when it looks like a spray.
  • 3Close off unused openings. We plug or cap other fixtures so water does not come up through a tub or floor drain.
  • 4Move people and pets out of the work area. A basement is the usual spot, so keep the stairs clear.
  • 5Keep electrical items off the floor. A wet floor and a power strip are a poor mix.

What moves the price of a jetting job

We give a clear price before work starts, and it depends on pipe size, how long the run is, how heavy the buildup is, where we can get in, and whether a camera pass before and after is part of the visit. Jetting a single branch is a different job from the whole main.

A camera look at the start, like a stand-alone sewer inspection, is often the best way to decide if jetting is worth paying for at all.
